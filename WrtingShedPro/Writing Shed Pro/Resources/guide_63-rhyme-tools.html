Rhyme Tools

Rhyme is a fundamental element of many poetry forms. Writing Shed Pro provides tools to help you identify, plan, and execute rhyme schemes.

Rhyme Scheme Display

When working with a form that has a specific rhyme scheme:

Viewing the Scheme

  • The expected rhyme scheme appears in the form reference panel
  • Each line is labeled (A, B, C, etc.)
  • Same letters should rhyme

Example: Shakespearean Sonnet

Line 1  (A)    When in disgrace with fortune and men's eyes

Line 2  (B)    I all alone beweep my outcast state


Line 3  (A)    And trouble deaf heaven with my bootless cries


Line 4  (B)    And look upon myself and curse my fate


...


Line 13 (G)    For thy sweet love remembered such wealth brings


Line 14 (G)    That then I scorn to change my state with kings

Types of Rhyme

Writing Shed Pro recognizes various rhyme types:

Perfect Rhyme

Words with identical endings:

  • cat/hat
  • love/dove
  • night/light

Near Rhyme (Slant Rhyme)

Words with similar but not identical sounds:

  • love/move
  • eyes/light
  • moon/on

Internal Rhyme

Rhymes within a line:

  • "Once upon a midnight dreary, while I pondered, weak and weary"

Finding Rhymes

When you need rhyming words:

Using Look Up

  • Select a word you need to rhyme with
  • Use the system Look Up feature
  • Explore dictionary and thesaurus options

    • Manual Approach

    Consider:

    • Perfect rhymes first
    • Slant rhymes for more options
    • Near rhymes for subtle effects

    Rhyme Scheme Tips

    Plan Ahead

    For complex schemes, sketch rhymes before writing:

  • Identify your end words for A lines
  • List possible B rhymes
  • Continue for each rhyme group

    • Avoid Forced Rhyme

    • Don't sacrifice meaning for rhyme
    • Slant rhymes are often better than awkward perfect rhymes
    • The best rhyme feels inevitable, not forced

    Consider End Sounds

    Some sounds offer more rhymes:

    • "ay" sounds (day, way, say, play...)
    • "ight" sounds (night, light, bright...)

    Difficult rhyming sounds:

    • "orange" (few true rhymes)
    • "month" (limited options)
    • "silver" (challenging)

    Rhyme in Different Forms

    Sonnets

    • Complex schemes require planning
    • 3 quatrains + couplet (Shakespearean)
    • Octave + sestet (Petrarchan)

    Villanelle

    • Only two rhyme sounds (A and B)
    • Repeated lines add structure
    • Plan carefully before writing

    Limerick

    • AABBA scheme
    • A lines typically longer
    • B lines short and punchy

    Free Verse

    • No required rhyme
    • Use rhyme for effect when desired
    • Internal rhyme creates subtle music

    Common Rhyming Challenges

    Overused Rhymes

    Avoid clichéd pairs:

    • love/dove/above
    • heart/apart
    • moon/June

    Inverted Syntax

    Don't twist sentences for rhyme:

    • "The house so white" ← awkward
    • "The white house" ← natural

    Rhyme Fatigue

    In long poems, vary:

    • Use slant rhymes occasionally
    • Place rhymes at unexpected spots
    • Give readers' ears a rest

    Building Your Rhyme Vocabulary

    Read Poetry

    Exposure builds your mental rhyme dictionary.

    Keep Lists

    Note good rhymes you discover.

    Practice

    Write exercises focusing on specific sounds.

    Thesaurus

    Find synonyms that offer better rhymes.
