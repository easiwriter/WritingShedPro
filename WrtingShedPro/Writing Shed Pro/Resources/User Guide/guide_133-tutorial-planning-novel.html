Tutorial: Planning and Writing a Novel

This tutorial walks you through the complete Fiction workflow in Writing Shed Pro — from setting up your project with characters and a story structure, to writing scenes, organizing chapters, and building toward a manuscript.

Time: 45–60 minutes
Project Type: Fiction (Novel)
Difficulty: Intermediate

What You'll Learn

  • Creating a Fiction (Novel) project with a story structure
  • Defining characters and locations
  • Creating plot elements tied to your story structure
  • Writing scenes and linking them to plot elements, characters, and locations
  • Organizing scenes into chapters
  • Viewing scenes grouped by chapter
  • Building body matter for manuscript assembly

Step 1: Create a Novel Project

  1. From the project list, tap +
  2. Name it "The Glass Mountain"
  3. Select Fiction as the project type
  4. Choose Novel as the fiction class
  5. Under Story Structure, select Three-Act Structure
  6. Tap Create

Your project opens with the Fiction folder structure: Scenes, Chapters, Plot Elements, Characters, Locations, and more.

The Three-Act Structure gives you predefined plot stages to attach scenes to: - Act 1 – Setup: Introduce world, characters, inciting incident - Act 2 – Confrontation: Rising action, complications, midpoint - Act 3 – Resolution: Climax, falling action, resolution

Step 2: Create Your Characters

Before writing a word, sketch your main characters. This helps you stay consistent throughout the novel.

Create the Protagonist

  1. Open the Characters folder
  2. Tap +
  3. Fill in the character form:
  4. Name: Elena Vasquez
  5. Role: Protagonist
  6. Details: Tall, dark hair, calloused hands from climbing. Determined, impatient, secretly afraid of heights. Grew up in a mining town and lost her brother to the mountain. Works as a mountain guide.
  7. Tap Create

Create Supporting Characters

Repeat for 4–5 more characters so you cover the core role set:

Name Role Key Trait
Marco Mentor Wise old guide, knows the mountain's secrets
Dr. Strand Antagonist Geologist who wants to mine the glass deposits
Lucia Ally Elena's childhood friend who chooses loyalty when stakes rise
Inez Skeptic Practical council member who challenges every mountain myth
Tomas Trickster Charismatic scavenger whose jokes and schemes expose hidden truths

Tip: You don't need every character before you start writing. Add new ones as the story reveals them.

Step 3: Create Your Locations

  1. Open the Locations folder
  2. Tap + for each location:

The Village

  • Name: Pueblo Cristal
  • Details: A small mountain village at the base of the Glass Mountain. Stone houses with glass-tile roofs catch the sunrise. Wind moves through narrow streets with distant rockfalls, and the air smells of pine smoke and mineral dust.

The Mountain

  • Name: The Glass Mountain
  • Details: A volcanic peak with exposed glass formations. Translucent cliff faces refract rainbow light. The mountain creaks underfoot, footsteps echo, and ozone lingers after lightning strikes.

Create as many locations as you need. Like characters, you can add more later.

Step 4: Set Up Plot Elements

Plot elements map your story against the chosen structure. With Three-Act Structure, you'll see predefined stages.

  1. Open Plot Elements
  2. You'll see the Three-Act stages listed
  3. For each stage, you can add notes about what happens:

Act 1 Stages

  • Ordinary World: Elena leads tourists up safe routes, avoiding the Glass Mountain's summit
  • Inciting Incident: Dr. Strand arrives with mining permits; the village council asks Elena to guide a survey team
  • First Turning Point: Elena discovers her brother's journal in the mountain caves

Act 2 Stages

  • Rising Action: The survey reveals the glass is alive — it grows, shifts, responds to sound
  • Midpoint: Elena can communicate with the mountain through vibration
  • Complications: Dr. Strand's team begins test drilling; the mountain retaliates with tremors

Act 3 Stages

  • Climax: Elena must climb to the summit to "speak" to the mountain before Strand detonates charges
  • Resolution: The mountain reveals it's been protecting the village for centuries; mining is abandoned

Step 5: Write Your First Scene

Now the planning pays off. Time to write.

  1. Open Scenes
  2. Tap +
  3. Name the scene: "Morning Route"
  4. Tap Create

The scene opens in the editor. Write your opening:

The trail was slick with last night's rain, and Elena placed each boot with the practiced care of someone who'd learned what carelessness cost...

Write freely. Don't worry about perfection — this is a draft.

Link Your Scene

After writing (or before, your choice):

  1. Open the scene's details or context menu
  2. Link to Plot Element: Select "Ordinary World"
  3. Link to Character: Select Elena and Marco
  4. Link to Location: Select "The Glass Mountain"

These links help you track which characters appear where, which plot points are covered, and which locations are used.

Step 6: Write More Scenes

Create and write several more scenes. Here's a sample set:

Scene Plot Element Characters Location
Morning Route Ordinary World Elena, Marco The Glass Mountain
Strand Arrives Inciting Incident Elena, Dr. Strand, Lucia Pueblo Cristal
The Journal First Turning Point Elena The Glass Mountain
Living Glass Rising Action Elena, Dr. Strand The Glass Mountain
The Vibration Midpoint Elena The Glass Mountain
Test Drilling Complications Elena, Dr. Strand, Marco The Glass Mountain
The Climb Climax Elena The Glass Mountain
The Mountain Speaks Resolution Elena, Lucia, Marco Pueblo Cristal

Each scene is a self-contained writing unit. Write them in any order — you don't have to work linearly.

Step 7: Organize Scenes into Chapters

Once you have scenes written, group them into chapters.

Create Chapters

  1. Open the Chapters folder
  2. Tap + to create your first chapter
  3. Name it: "Chapter 1: The Guide"
  4. Create more chapters as needed

Assign Scenes to Chapters

  1. Open a chapter
  2. Tap Add Scenes
  3. Select the scenes that belong in this chapter
  4. Confirm

Or from the scene side: 1. Open the scene's details 2. Tap Chapter 3. Select the chapter this scene belongs to

Reorder Scenes Within Chapters

  1. Open a chapter
  2. Tap Edit
  3. Drag scenes into reading order
  4. Tap Done

Example Chapter Plan

Chapter Scenes
Chapter 1: The Guide Morning Route, Strand Arrives
Chapter 2: The Journal The Journal
Chapter 3: Living Glass Living Glass, The Vibration
Chapter 4: The Stakes Test Drilling
Chapter 5: The Summit The Climb, The Mountain Speaks

Step 8: View Scenes Grouped by Chapter

In the Scenes view, you can see your scenes organized by their chapter assignment:

  1. Open Scenes
  2. Scenes appear in collapsible groups by chapter
  3. Expand or collapse each chapter group
  4. Scenes not yet assigned to a chapter appear in an "Unassigned" group

This view gives you an overview of your novel's structure at a glance. You can spot gaps (chapters with only one scene) or bottlenecks (chapters that are too long).

Step 9: Add Chapters to Body Matter

To assemble the manuscript:

  1. Open Manuscript → Body Matter
  2. Tap + to add chapters
  3. Add your chapters in reading order
  4. Each chapter expands to show its scenes

The body matter defines the final manuscript order. Reorder chapters here if needed.

Step 10: Check Your Progress

Use the project overview to see where you stand:

  • Characters: Are all major characters appearing in scenes?
  • Locations: Are all locations being used?
  • Plot Elements: Are all story stages covered by at least one scene?
  • Chapters: Are scenes assigned and ordered?

Write more scenes to fill gaps in your structure.

Tips for Novel Writing

  • Don't write linearly: Jump to the scene that excites you most
  • Link as you go: Connecting scenes to plot elements reveals gaps early
  • One scene, one purpose: If a scene doesn't advance plot or character, cut it
  • Characters evolve: Update character details as the story develops
  • Chapters are flexible: Reorganize scenes between chapters as the story takes shape
