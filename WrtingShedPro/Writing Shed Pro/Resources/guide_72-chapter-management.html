Chapter Management

In Novel projects, chapters organize your scenes into a reading order. This guide covers creating, organizing, and working with chapters.

Understanding Chapters

Chapters in Writing Shed Pro:

  • Are containers for scenes
  • Determine the order scenes appear in the manuscript
  • Can include multiple scenes
  • Live in the Chapters folder

Creating Chapters

From the Chapters Folder

  • Open the Chapters folder
  • Tap +
  • Enter a chapter title (e.g., "Chapter 1: The Beginning")
  • Tap Create

    • When to Create Chapters

    • After writing several scenes, or
    • Before writing (if you outline), or
    • During revision as structure emerges

    There's no wrong time—do what fits your process.

    Adding Scenes to Chapters

    From the Scene

  • Open a scene in Draft or Ready
  • Access scene settings
  • Select Add to Chapter
  • Choose the chapter

    • From the Chapter

  • Open a chapter
  • Tap Add Scene
  • Select from available scenes

    • Drag and Drop

    On iPad/Mac:

    • Drag scenes from Draft/Ready
    • Drop onto a chapter
    • Scene is added to that chapter

    Scene Order Within Chapters

    Scenes appear in the chapter in a specific order:

    Reordering Scenes

  • Open the chapter
  • Tap Edit
  • Drag scenes using the handles
  • Tap Done

    • The order determines how scenes appear in the assembled manuscript.

    Renaming Chapters

  • Open the Chapters folder
  • Long-press the chapter
  • Select Rename
  • Enter the new title
  • Tap Done

    • Deleting Chapters

  • Swipe left on the chapter
  • Tap Delete
  • Confirm
    • Note: Deleting a chapter does not delete the scenes—they remain in Draft/Ready. Only the chapter container is removed.

    Naming Conventions

    Some options:

    • "Chapter 1", "Chapter 2"... (simple)
    • "Chapter 1: The Storm" (numbered with title)
    • "The Storm" (title only)
    • "1" (minimalist)

    Choose a style and be consistent.

    Chapter Length

    There's no rule, but consider:

    • Reader attention spans
    • Natural story breaks
    • Pacing and rhythm

    Short chapters create urgency; long chapters allow immersion.

    When Scenes Belong to No Chapter

    Scenes don't have to be in chapters:

    • Keep works-in-progress in Draft
    • Use chapters only for manuscript organization
    • It's okay to have "loose" scenes

    Multi-Chapter Scenes

    A scene can only belong to one chapter. If content spans chapters:

    • Split into multiple scenes
    • Or place in the chapter where it primarily belongs

    Viewing Chapter Contents

    When you open a chapter:

    • See all scenes in order
    • View word counts
    • Access each scene for editing

    Chapters and Manuscript

    When you assemble or export your manuscript:

    • Chapters appear in order
    • Scenes within chapters appear in order
    • Chapter titles become headings

    See Manuscript Formatting for export details.

    Troubleshooting

    Scene Missing from Chapter

    • Check if it was removed
    • Look in Draft/Ready folders
    • Scenes must be added explicitly

    Wrong Scene Order

    • Open chapter in Edit mode
    • Drag to correct order

    Chapter Won't Delete

    • Ensure you have access
    • Check for app issues (restart if needed)
