# Verse Highlighting

For poets working with metered verse, Writing Shed Pro provides stress pattern analysis to help you maintain consistent rhythm.

## Understanding Meter

Meter is the pattern of stressed and unstressed syllables in a line of poetry:

- **Unstressed** (˘): Lighter, shorter sounds
- **Stressed** (´): Heavier, emphasized sounds

### Common Meters

| Meter | Pattern | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|
| **Iambic** | ˘ ´ | "a-LONE" |
| **Trochaic** | ´ ˘ | "GAR-den" |
| **Anapestic** | ˘ ˘ ´ | "in-ter-VENE" |
| **Dactylic** | ´ ˘ ˘ | "MER-ri-ly" |
| **Spondaic** | ´ ´ | "HEART-BREAK" |

### Feet Per Line

The number of feet determines the line length:
- Trimeter: 3 feet
- Tetrameter: 4 feet
- Pentameter: 5 feet
- Hexameter: 6 feet

**Example**: Iambic pentameter = 5 iambic feet (˘ ´ ˘ ´ ˘ ´ ˘ ´ ˘ ´)

## How Stress Detection Works

Writing Shed Pro uses:

### Dictionary-Based Analysis
- Pronunciation dictionaries provide stress markers for known words
- **American English**: CMU Pronouncing Dictionary
- **British English**: Specialized UK pronunciation dictionary
- High accuracy for standard pronunciation in your selected dialect

### Dialect Selection
Stress patterns can differ significantly between American and British English. See [Syllable Counting](02-syllable-counting.md#selecting-your-dialect) for how to switch between dialects.

#### Stress Differences by Dialect
| Word | American | British |
|------|----------|---------|
| advertisement | ad-ver-TISE-ment | ad-VER-tis-ment |
| controversy | CON-tro-ver-sy | con-TROV-er-sy |
| primarily | pry-MARE-uh-lee | PRY-muh-ruh-lee |
| research | REE-search / ree-SEARCH | REE-search |

The stress pattern display automatically reflects your chosen dialect.

### Contextual Estimation
- Some words change stress based on usage
- "record" (noun: RE-cord) vs. "record" (verb: re-CORD)
- Algorithm uses common patterns

### Limitations
- Estimates, not certainties
- Pronunciation varies by dialect
- Poetic emphasis may differ from speech

## Working with Stress Patterns

### Verifying Meter
For formal verse:
1. Write your line
2. Check the stress display
3. Read aloud to confirm
4. Adjust problem areas

### Common Issues

**Wrong stress on a word**
- Try a synonym
- Rearrange the phrase
- Consider if the "error" works poetically

**Extra or missing syllables**
- Adjust word choices
- Use elision when appropriate
- Consider metrical substitution

### Metrical Substitution
Poets regularly substitute feet for effect:
- A trochee in iambic verse for emphasis
- A spondee for weight
- A pyrrhic (˘ ˘) for lightness

Good poetry varies the beat—perfect regularity can sound mechanical.

## Stress in Different Forms

### Sonnets
Traditionally iambic pentameter:
- 10 syllables per line
- Alternating unstressed-stressed
- Substitutions common and encouraged

### Blank Verse
Iambic pentameter without rhyme:
- Shakespeare's plays
- Milton's Paradise Lost
- Flexibility within the pattern

### Ballad Meter
Alternating tetrameter and trimeter:
- Common in folk songs
- "Common meter" in hymns

### Free Verse
No required meter:
- Use stress for emphasis
- Create rhythmic phrases
- Contrast regular and irregular lines

## Tips for Metered Verse

### Read Shakespeare
The master of iambic pentameter. Note where he follows and breaks the pattern.

### Read Aloud
Always. The stress display is a guide—your voice is the test.

### Embrace Variation
Perfect meter sounds like a metronome. Variation creates life.

### Trust Your Ear
If it sounds right, it probably is—regardless of what the analysis shows.

### Learn Natural Stress
English has natural rhythms. Learn which words are typically stressed.

## Disabling Stress Display

If the stress marks distract you:
1. Toggle off in poem settings
2. Re-enable when checking meter
3. Use as a tool, not a constant presence
