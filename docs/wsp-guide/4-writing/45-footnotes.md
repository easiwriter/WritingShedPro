# Footnotes

Footnotes let you add supplementary information without interrupting the flow of your text. They're essential for academic writing, useful for annotations, and can add depth to fiction and poetry.

## Understanding Footnotes

A footnote consists of two parts:
1. **Marker**: A superscript number in your text (¹, ², ³)
2. **Content**: The actual note text, displayed at the page bottom

Writing Shed Pro numbers footnotes automatically.

## Inserting a Footnote

1. Place cursor where you want the footnote marker
2. Tap the **⊕** (Insert) button in the toolbar
3. Select **Footnote**
4. Type your footnote text in the editor that appears
5. Tap **Save** or **Done**

The marker appears in your text as a superscript number.

## Editing a Footnote

### Quick Edit
1. Tap the footnote marker in your text
2. The footnote editor opens
3. Make your changes
4. Tap **Save**

### From Footnotes List
1. Open the Insert menu and choose **Show Footnotes**
2. Find the footnote you want to edit
3. Tap to edit
4. Save your changes

## Viewing All Footnotes

To see all footnotes in your document:

1. Open the Insert menu
2. Select **Show Footnotes**

The list shows:
- Footnote number
- Preview of content
- Location in document

Tap any footnote to jump to its marker in the text.

## Deleting a Footnote

### From the Text
1. Select the footnote marker
2. Press Delete/Backspace

### From the Footnotes List
1. Open **Footnotes...**
2. Swipe left on the footnote
3. Tap **Delete**
4. Confirm

When you delete a footnote, remaining footnotes renumber automatically.

## Automatic Numbering

Footnotes are numbered sequentially:
- First footnote is ¹
- Second is ²
- And so on...

### Renumbering
When you add or delete footnotes:
- All numbers update automatically
- References stay accurate
- No manual renumbering needed

## Footnotes vs Endnotes

Writing Shed Pro supports two display modes:

### Footnotes Mode
- Notes appear at the bottom of the page where referenced
- Traditional academic and book style
- Better for frequent reference

### Endnotes Mode
- All notes appear at the end of the document
- Endnotes are collected in the manuscript back matter
- Cleaner page layout
- Common in some publication styles

### Switching Modes
1. Open the Insert menu and choose **Footnote** or **Endnote** when adding a note
2. Use your manuscript settings to control whether notes are displayed as footnotes or gathered as endnotes in back matter


The content doesn't change—only where it appears.

## Footnotes in Pagination View

In pagination view (full-page layout):
- Footnotes appear at page bottom
- A separator line divides text from footnotes
- Long footnotes may continue on the next page
- Professional typesetting rules apply

### Pagination Rules
- Minimum 2 lines of body text above footnotes
- Long footnotes split with continuation
- Proper spacing maintained

## Footnote Formatting

Footnote text uses the **Footnote** paragraph style:
- Typically smaller than body text
- May have different indentation
- Customize in Stylesheet Editor

### Customizing Footnote Style
1. Open Stylesheet Editor
2. Find the Footnote style
3. Adjust font, size, spacing as needed
4. Save

## Best Practices

### Keep Notes Concise
Footnotes should supplement, not replace, main text. If a note is very long, consider incorporating the information elsewhere.

### Number Placement
Place footnote markers:
- After punctuation when possible
- At natural pause points
- Consistently throughout

### Use Appropriately
- Academic: Citations, clarifications
- Fiction: Historical context, translations
- Poetry: References, variations

### Review Before Export
Check footnotes in pagination view before exporting to ensure they appear correctly.

## Troubleshooting

### Footnotes Not Showing in Export
- Ensure export format supports footnotes
- Check pagination view first
- PDF and RTF support footnotes

### Wrong Numbers
- Delete and re-add if persistent
- Check for duplicate markers
- Renumbering is automatic

### Marker Hard to See
- Markers are styled as small superscripts
- Zoom in if needed
- They're tappable despite small size
