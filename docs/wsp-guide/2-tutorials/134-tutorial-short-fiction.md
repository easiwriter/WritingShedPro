# Tutorial: Writing and Collecting Short Fiction

This tutorial guides you through creating a Short Fiction project in Writing Shed Pro — writing individual stories, organizing scenes, and assembling a collection for submission or export.

**Time**: 20–30 minutes  
**Project Type**: Fiction (Short Fiction)  
**Difficulty**: Beginner–Intermediate  
**Best for**: Short story writers, flash fiction, anthologies, story collections

## What You'll Learn

- Creating a Short Fiction project
- Writing scenes as the basic building blocks
- Grouping scenes into stories
- Using characters and locations across stories
- Assembling a short fiction manuscript
- Exporting individual stories or an entire collection

## Step 1: Create a Short Fiction Project

1. From the project list, tap **+**
2. Name it **"City Stories"** (we'll write a small collection of urban-themed stories)
3. Select **Fiction** as the project type
4. Choose **Short Fiction** as the fiction class
5. Tap **Create**

The project opens with the Short Fiction folder structure. The key areas are:

- **Scenes** — where you write
- **Stories** — containers that group scenes into individual stories
- **Characters** — people who appear across your stories
- **Locations** — settings you can reuse
- **Manuscript** — for assembling your final output

Short Fiction uses a simpler structure than Novel: scenes are your primary writing unit, and stories are optional containers that group related scenes together.

## Step 2: Plan Your Stories

Before writing, sketch out a few stories. For this tutorial, we'll create three:

| Story | Scenes | Summary |
|-------|--------|---------|
| The Last Bus | 3 scenes | A commuter's unexpected late-night encounter |
| Rooftop Garden | 2 scenes | Two neighbours connect over a shared garden |
| The Waiting Room | 1 scene | A single continuous scene in a hospital waiting room |

Notice that stories can have multiple scenes or just one. A flash fiction piece might be a single scene; a longer story might have three or four scenes representing shifts in time, location, or perspective.

## Step 3: Create Your First Scenes

1. Open the **Scenes** folder
2. Tap **+** to create a new scene
3. Name it: **"Missing the Express"**
4. Add a synopsis: *"Alex runs for the last express train and misses it — forced to wait for the local"*
5. Tap **Create**

Create a few more scenes:
- **"The Platform Stranger"** — *"A conversation with a mysterious traveller on the empty platform"*
- **"Arrival"** — *"Alex arrives home changed by the encounter"*
- **"Seeds"** — *"Mei discovers a rooftop garden she never knew existed"*
- **"Growing"** — *"The garden becomes a meeting point; an unlikely friendship forms"*
- **"Three Hours"** — *"A father waits for news, watching others in the waiting room"*

## Step 4: Write a Scene

Open **"Missing the Express"** and start writing. A few tips for writing scenes in Short Fiction mode:

### Scene Settings
Each scene has metadata you can set:
- **Synopsis**: A brief summary (you added this when creating)
- **Status**: Draft, Ready, or Set Aside
- **POV Character**: Whose perspective drives this scene (set up characters in Step 6)

### Writing Tips for Short Fiction
- **Start late, end early**: Short stories thrive on economy — drop readers into the action
- **One scene, one shift**: Each scene should mark a change — in time, place, mood, or understanding
- **Use the synopsis**: Jot down the scene's purpose before you write it; this keeps you focused

Write a few paragraphs for "Missing the Express." Don't worry about perfection — you can revise later.

## Step 5: Group Scenes into Stories

Now organize your scenes into their stories.

### Create Stories
1. Open the **Stories** folder
2. Tap **+**
3. Name it: **"The Last Bus"** (we'll keep the working title even though it started as a train)
4. Add a synopsis: *"A missed train leads to an unexpected connection"*
5. Tap **Create**

Create two more stories:
- **"Rooftop Garden"** — *"Two neighbours find common ground above the city"*
- **"The Waiting Room"** — *"Time stretches in a hospital waiting room"*

### Assign Scenes to Stories

#### From the Scene
1. Open a scene (e.g., "Missing the Express")
2. Access scene settings
3. Select **Add to Story**
4. Choose **The Last Bus**

Assign all scenes to their stories:
- "Missing the Express," "The Platform Stranger," "Arrival" → **The Last Bus**
- "Seeds," "Growing" → **Rooftop Garden**
- "Three Hours" → **The Waiting Room**

#### Reorder Scenes Within a Story
1. Open a story (e.g., **The Last Bus**)
2. Tap **Edit**
3. Drag scenes into the correct reading order
4. Tap **Done**

**Remember**: A scene can only belong to one story. If you need the same content in two places, create separate scenes.

## Step 6: Add Characters and Locations

Short Fiction projects include Characters and Locations folders, which help you track details across your collection.

### Create Characters
1. Open the **Characters** folder
2. Tap **+**
3. Name: **"Alex"**
4. Set details:
   - **Role**: Protagonist
   - **Notes**: *"Overworked office drone, mid-30s, takes the same train every night"*
5. Tap **Create**

Add more characters:
- **Mei** — Protagonist of "Rooftop Garden"
- **David** — The father in "The Waiting Room"
- **The Traveller** — A mysterious figure in "The Last Bus"

### Create Locations
1. Open the **Locations** folder
2. Tap **+**
3. Name: **"Platform 4"**
4. Notes: *"Empty suburban platform, fluorescent lighting, one broken bench"*

Add more:
- **The Rooftop** — *"A hidden garden on a six-storey apartment building"*
- **City General Hospital** — *"Beige walls, plastic chairs, a muted television"*

### Link Characters to Scenes
Set the POV character for each scene in its scene settings. This helps you track which characters drive which parts of the story.

## Step 7: Mark Scenes as Ready

As you finish writing and revising each scene:

1. Open the scene
2. Access scene settings
3. Change the status from **Draft** to **Ready**

Only scenes marked as **Ready** are included in manuscript assembly. This lets you keep experimental scenes or fragments in your project without them appearing in your export.

**Tip**: Use **Set Aside** for scenes you've written but decided not to include — they stay in the project but are clearly flagged as inactive.

## Step 8: Assemble the Manuscript

### Add Stories to Body Matter
1. Open **Manuscript** → **Body Matter**
2. Add your stories in reading order:
   - The Last Bus
   - Rooftop Garden
   - The Waiting Room

Each story expands to show its scenes. The manuscript will include all Ready scenes in the order they appear within each story.

### Scene Breaks
Between scenes within a story, Writing Shed Pro inserts scene breaks (typically represented by a blank line or centered marker like `* * *`). Between stories, a new section or page break begins.

### Front and Back Matter
For a collection, consider adding:
- **Title Page**: Collection title and author name
- **Table of Contents**: Auto-generated from story titles
- **Author's Note**: Optional back matter

## Step 9: Export Your Collection

### Export the Full Collection
1. Open **Export Options**
2. Select your format (PDF, RTF)
3. Configure settings:
   - **Include Table of Contents**: Useful for collections with multiple stories
   - **Page breaks between stories**: Recommended
4. Preview and export

### Export a Single Story
If you want to export just one story (e.g., for submission to a magazine):
1. Navigate to the story
2. Use the export option for that story only
3. This includes only the scenes within that story

This is particularly useful when submitting individual stories to different publications. See the **[Submitting Your Work](137-tutorial-submitting-work.md)** tutorial for the full submission workflow.

## What You've Learned

- Creating a Short Fiction project and understanding its structure
- Writing scenes as the basic unit of fiction
- Grouping scenes into stories
- Using characters and locations to track details across a collection
- Setting scene statuses to control manuscript inclusion
- Assembling a manuscript with multiple stories
- Exporting the full collection or individual stories

## Tips for Short Fiction Writers

- **One project, many stories**: A Short Fiction project is ideal for a themed collection — keep related stories together
- **Scenes are flexible**: A flash fiction piece (under 1,000 words) might be a single scene; a novelette might have eight
- **Stories are optional**: If you're writing standalone flash pieces, you can skip stories entirely and work directly with scenes
- **Use synopses**: Scene and story synopses help you see the shape of your collection at a glance
- **Status workflow**: Use Draft → Ready → Set Aside as a simple editorial pipeline
- **Characters recur**: If a character appears in multiple stories (linked stories or a story cycle), create them once and reference them everywhere
