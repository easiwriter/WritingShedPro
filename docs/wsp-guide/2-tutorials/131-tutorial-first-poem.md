# Tutorial: Your First Poem — From Blank Page to Finished Sonnet

This tutorial walks you through writing a Shakespearean sonnet in Writing Shed Pro, using the poetry tools to guide your meter and rhyme. By the end, you'll have a polished poem and know how to use syllable counting, stress analysis, and form templates.

**Time**: 20–30 minutes  
**Project Type**: Poetry  
**Difficulty**: Beginner

## What You'll Learn

- Creating a Poetry project
- Using a form template (Shakespearean Sonnet)
- Writing with the syllable counter
- Checking stress patterns and meter
- Using the rhyme tool
- Moving a finished poem from Draft to Ready

## Step 1: Create a Poetry Project

1. From the project list, tap **+**
2. Name it **"Sonnet Practice"**
3. Select **Poetry** as the project type
4. Tap **Create**

Your project opens with the standard Poetry folder structure: Poems, Collections, Submissions, Manuscript, Research, and more.

## Step 2: Create a Poem with a Form Template

1. Open the **Poems** folder
2. Tap **+** to create a new file
3. Name it **"First Light"** (or any title you like)
4. In the **Poetry Form** dropdown, select **Shakespearean Sonnet**
5. Tap **Create**

The poem opens in the editor. You'll see the form template as a guide — it shows the expected structure: 14 lines, organized as three quatrains and a closing couplet.

## Step 3: Understand the Sonnet Form

Before writing, look at what the template tells you:

| Section | Lines | Rhyme Scheme |
|---------|-------|-------------|
| Quatrain 1 | Lines 1–4 | ABAB |
| Quatrain 2 | Lines 5–8 | CDCD |
| Quatrain 3 | Lines 9–12 | EFEF |
| Couplet | Lines 13–14 | GG |

Each line should have **10 syllables** in **iambic pentameter** (five pairs of unstressed-stressed syllables: da-DUM da-DUM da-DUM da-DUM da-DUM).

## Step 4: Write Your First Quatrain

Start typing your first four lines. Don't worry about perfection — get your ideas down first.

Try something like:

```
The morning breaks across the silent hill
And golden light spills through the frosted pane
A robin sings beside the windowsill
While mist dissolves like half-remembered rain
```

### Check Your Syllable Count

Look at the syllable counter in the toolbar. Each line should show **10** syllables. If a line is off:

- Too many? Try a shorter synonym
- Too few? Expand a phrase or add an adjective

The counter updates in real time as you type.

### Check Your Stress Pattern

Tap the **stress pattern** button (the meter icon) to see the stressed and unstressed syllables highlighted:

- Unstressed syllables appear in normal weight
- Stressed syllables appear **bold** or highlighted

For iambic pentameter, look for an alternating pattern. Don't worry if it's not perfectly regular — Shakespeare himself varied his meter for effect.

## Step 5: Use the Rhyme Tool

Now check that your rhyme scheme works:

1. Tap the **Rhyme** tool in the toolbar
2. Select a word at the end of line 1 (e.g., "hill")
3. The rhyme panel shows matching words

You need line 3 to rhyme with line 1 (A rhyme), and line 4 to rhyme with line 2 (B rhyme).

If you're stuck for a rhyme, the rhyme tool suggests options. You can tap a suggestion to see it in context.

## Step 6: Complete the Remaining Sections

Continue writing the second quatrain (lines 5–8) with a new CDCD rhyme scheme, the third quatrain (lines 9–12) with EFEF, and finally the closing couplet (lines 13–14) with GG.

**Tips for each section:**

- **Quatrain 2**: Develop or complicate the image from Quatrain 1
- **Quatrain 3**: Introduce a turn or shift in perspective
- **Couplet**: Resolve the poem with a memorable closing thought

Keep checking your syllable count and stress patterns as you go.

## Step 7: Review Using Verse Highlighting

Enable **Verse Highlighting** to see your poem's structure color-coded:

1. Tap the verse highlighting toggle in the toolbar
2. Each rhyme group is highlighted in a different color
3. Lines that match the expected syllable count show a green indicator
4. Lines that are over or under show a warning

This gives you a visual overview of where the form is working and where it needs attention.

## Step 8: Polish and Revise

Read through your complete sonnet. Check for:

- **Syllable counts**: All 14 lines should be 10 syllables
- **Rhyme scheme**: ABAB CDCD EFEF GG
- **Meter**: Generally iambic, with deliberate variations
- **Volta**: A clear turn at line 9 or 13
- **Imagery**: Consistent and vivid

Make edits. The syllable counter and stress display update as you type. The rhyme tool helps you find alternatives when a rhyme isn't working.

## Step 9: Mark as Ready

When you're satisfied with your sonnet:

1. Tap **Edit** in the file list toolbar
2. Select your poem
3. Tap **Change Status** (🔁)
4. Choose **Ready**

Your poem moves from Draft to Ready status, signaling that it's complete.

## What You've Learned

- Creating a Poetry project and choosing a form template
- Writing with real-time syllable counting
- Using stress pattern display to check meter
- Finding rhymes with the rhyme tool
- Using verse highlighting for structural overview
- Managing workflow status (Draft → Ready)
