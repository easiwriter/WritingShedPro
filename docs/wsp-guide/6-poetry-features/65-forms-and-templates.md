# Forms and Templates

Writing Shed Pro includes templates for over 30 traditional poetry forms. This guide lists available forms and explains how to use templates effectively.

## Using Form Templates

### Creating a Poem with a Template
1. In a Poetry project, open Draft folder
2. Tap **+** to create a new poem
3. Enter a title
4. Select a form from the dropdown
5. Tap **Create**

The poem opens with the form's structure as a guide.

### What Templates Provide
- Line placeholders
- Rhyme scheme indication
- Syllable count targets
- Form reference information

### Templates as Guides
Templates show structure—they don't enforce it. You can:
- Follow the template exactly
- Adapt it to your needs
- Delete placeholders and write freely

## Japanese Forms

### Haiku
- **Lines**: 3
- **Syllables**: 5-7-5 (17 total)
- **Subject**: Nature, seasons, single moment
- **Special**: No rhyme, no title traditionally

### Tanka
- **Lines**: 5
- **Syllables**: 5-7-5-7-7 (31 total)
- **Structure**: Haiku-like opening + emotional reflection
- **Special**: Pivot line creates shift

### Senryū
- **Lines**: 3
- **Syllables**: 5-7-5
- **Subject**: Human nature, humor, irony
- **Special**: Haiku structure, different focus

### Cinquain
- **Lines**: 5
- **Syllables**: 2-4-6-8-2 (22 total)
- **Structure**: Diamond-shaped syllable pattern
- **Special**: American form inspired by haiku

## Sonnets

### Shakespearean (English)
- **Lines**: 14
- **Meter**: Iambic pentameter
- **Rhyme**: ABAB CDCD EFEF GG
- **Structure**: 3 quatrains + couplet

### Petrarchan (Italian)
- **Lines**: 14
- **Meter**: Iambic pentameter
- **Rhyme**: ABBAABBA CDECDE (or CDCDCD)
- **Structure**: Octave + sestet with volta

### Spenserian Sonnet
- **Lines**: 14
- **Meter**: Iambic pentameter
- **Rhyme**: ABAB BCBC CDCD EE
- **Structure**: Interlocking rhymes + couplet

## French Forms

### Villanelle
- **Lines**: 19
- **Structure**: 5 tercets + 1 quatrain
- **Rhyme**: ABA ABA ABA ABA ABA ABAA
- **Special**: Lines 1 and 3 repeat as refrains

### Pantoum
- **Lines**: Variable (multiples of 4)
- **Structure**: Interlocking quatrains
- **Rhyme**: ABAB (lines 2,4 become 1,3 of next stanza)
- **Special**: Last stanza uses first lines

### Rondeau
- **Lines**: 15
- **Structure**: 3 stanzas (5-4-6 lines)
- **Rhyme**: AABBA AABR AABBAR
- **Special**: Rentrement (refrain from opening)

### Ballade
- **Lines**: 28 (3 stanzas of 8 + 4-line envoi)
- **Rhyme**: ABABBCBC (envoi: BCBC)
- **Special**: Refrain ends each stanza

### Triolet
- **Lines**: 8
- **Rhyme**: ABaAabAB
- **Special**: Lines 1, 4, 7 identical; Lines 2, 8 identical

## Welsh Forms

### Cywydd
- **Lines**: Couplets
- **Syllables**: 7 per line
- **Rhyme**: One stressed, one unstressed per couplet
- **Special**: Complex sound patterns (cynghanedd)

### Englyn
- **Lines**: 4
- **Syllables**: 30 total (10-6-7-7 traditional)
- **Special**: Pivot in first line

## English Forms

### Limerick
- **Lines**: 5
- **Rhyme**: AABBA
- **Meter**: Anapestic
- **Special**: Humorous content

### Ballad
- **Lines**: Quatrains
- **Rhyme**: ABAB or ABCB
- **Meter**: Alternating tetrameter/trimeter
- **Special**: Narrative, often with refrain

### Blank Verse
- **Lines**: Variable
- **Meter**: Iambic pentameter
- **Rhyme**: None
- **Special**: Drama, epic, meditative poetry

### Heroic Couplets
- **Lines**: Couplets
- **Meter**: Iambic pentameter
- **Rhyme**: AA BB CC...
- **Special**: Augustan poetry, wit

## Italian Forms

### Ottava Rima
- **Lines**: 8
- **Meter**: Iambic pentameter
- **Rhyme**: ABABABCC
- **Special**: Six alternating rhymes + couplet

### Terza Rima
- **Lines**: Variable (tercets)
- **Meter**: Iambic pentameter
- **Rhyme**: ABA BCB CDC...
- **Special**: Interlocking rhymes, Dante's form

### Spenserian Stanza
- **Lines**: 9
- **Meter**: Iambic pentameter + alexandrine
- **Rhyme**: ABABBCBCC
- **Special**: Final line has 12 syllables

## Modern Forms

### Free Verse
- No fixed structure
- No required rhyme or meter
- Emphasis on rhythm, image, meaning
- Most common contemporary form

### Prose Poetry
- Block paragraphs
- No line breaks
- Poetic language and imagery
- Blurs poetry/prose boundary

### Concrete/Visual Poetry
- Shape matters
- Words form images
- Spatial arrangement significant

## Complex Forms

### Sestina
- **Lines**: 39 (6 six-line stanzas + 3-line envoi)
- **Rhyme**: None (word repetition)
- **Special**: 6 end words rotate in set pattern

### Crown of Sonnets
- **Lines**: 98 (7 linked sonnets)
- **Structure**: Each sonnet's last line becomes next's first
- **Special**: Final sonnet uses all first lines

### Ghazal
- **Lines**: Couplets (5-15)
- **Rhyme**: AA BA CA DA...
- **Special**: Refrain + signature in final couplet

## Working with Templates

### Start Simple
Begin with shorter forms (haiku, limerick) before tackling villanelles or sestinas.

### Read Examples
Study published poems in your chosen form. Understanding comes from exposure.

### Break the Rules Knowingly
Learn the form first, then decide when to deviate. Informed rule-breaking is creative; ignorant rule-breaking is sloppy.

### Use Templates Repeatedly
Write many poems in a form to truly learn it. One sonnet is practice; twenty is mastery.
